# Review and polish: report and plan, 18 November 2026

> **Outcome, added the same day after the build.** The plan below was approved and built by Opus
> builders in three rounds, then read by three fresh reviewers and run on the real tools. What was
> done, what was measured and what is still unchecked is in [Outcome](#outcome) at the end. The
> report itself is left as it was written, before any code changed.

Nothing in the app was changed for this report. It covers the whole app at commit `220cc0e` (today's
new left panel included), read by nine auditors that each owned one area, with a tenth pass by me to
check their main claims against the code. The work itself waits for your go-ahead.

## In short

- **The app is sound.** Type checks pass in all three packages. 1,261 of 1,267 engine tests and all
  24 desktop tests pass. The six that failed are timing tests that fail only when the machine is
  busy: each passes when run alone, on today's code and on the commit before it.
- **No new permission hole and no way to lose work was found.** The one access finding is a rule that
  is too strict, not too loose (B8).
- **168 findings, about 155 after merging the ones two auditors both found.** 6 high, 44 medium, 118
  low. All six high ones are about speed, and five of the six are in the interface.
- **The biggest win is how the app feels while an agent is answering.** Today, one streamed frame of a
  reply redraws every open panel in the dock (hidden tabs too), one step of any running session
  redraws the whole shell, and clicking a session you already had open fetches its whole thread again
  and freezes its live text until that returns. These are a handful of bounded fixes, not a rewrite.
- **About 600 lines of CSS, some twenty unused components and exports, seven engine calls nothing
  calls, and two window materials nothing can select are dead** and can go.

## The plan

Built the way you asked for the last big jobs: I write one brief per builder from the auditor files,
Opus builders write the code, each owning its own files so none overlap, and I read every diff and
send defects back. No formatter, no commits unless you say, never your real accounts.

**Step 0. Measure first (me, about an hour).** An isolated dev build on the Demo provider, timed
over CDP: frames dropped while a reply streams with Review open, time to switch session, a frame
drag in Liner Design with 30 frames, launch to first paint, main chunk size. Without this, "faster"
is a claim; with it, each fix in A and D gets a before and an after.

## Decisions I need from you

1. **How much to do.** I recommend rounds 1 to 3 as written. The lighter choice is round 1 only
   (feel and bugs), leaving deletion and refactors for later.
2. **B8, the ACP read rule.** I recommend letting an ACP agent read your attachments and the folders

4. **B11, what "Needs you" means.** I recommend one meaning everywhere: asking you something, or

5. **B7, Stop.** I recommend a 15 second limit: a tool that has not ended its turn by then has its

6. **E4 and E6.** I recommend deleting the Glass and Vibrancy materials (nothing can select them)

7. **E5, the seven unused engine calls.** I recommend deleting the three nothing calls and keeping

8. **E7, disk.** Not mine to delete. If you want, I list the old releases in `build/` by date and
